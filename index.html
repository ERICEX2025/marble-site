The app

Three screens. Nothing you have to learn.

No streaks, no badges, no ring to close. Marble asks two things of you a day and spends the rest of its time working out whether the number it gave you was right.

Where the number comes from

We show you the arithmetic, including the shaky part.

Your target is built from your body — the equation has been the standard since 1990. But the largest input is a question about how active you are, and nobody answers that well. Most people overshoot it. That one line can move your target by a few hundred calories.

So Marble says which line it isn't sure about, rather than handing you a number and letting you assume it's measured. Everything after that is the app finding out.

The part nobody explains

Your scale is going to lie to you. Constantly.

Salt, water, carbohydrate and what's still in your gut move the number by a few pounds overnight. Real fat loss is about half a pound a week. The noise is several times larger than the signal — which is why people quit three days into something that was working.

The part no other tracker does

Three weeks in, it tells you it was wrong.

Once your weigh-ins have said something the formula couldn't, Marble compares what your weight actually did against what the target was built to produce — and changes the target to match. Not a nudge you have to interpret. A new number, and a plain sentence about why the first one missed.

“We were wrong. Here's the real number.”

From then on the figure on your home screen stops saying estimated and starts saying measured. That word changing is the whole product.

How it works

A guess, two habits, and a correction.

1

It starts with a guess

A minute of questions — height, weight, age, what you're after. Marble works out a target and shows you the arithmetic that produced it, including which line is the shakiest. It's an estimate, and it says so.

2

You feed it two things

Photograph what you eat — the dish, the calories, the protein. Then step on the scale each morning. Ten seconds, one number, and the app never judges you on a single reading.

3

It corrects itself

After about three weeks your weigh-ins have said something the formula couldn't: what you actually burn. Marble changes the target to match, and tells you plainly that its first guess was wrong.

Why it's different

Every calorie app is guessing. Marble is the one that admits it, measures how wrong it was, and fixes the number.

The target you're given on day one comes from an equation that's been the standard since 1990 — and it's still only accurate to within a few hundred calories, because the biggest input is a question about how active you are that nobody answers well. Marble treats that as a starting guess rather than a verdict. Your weigh-ins do the rest.

Which is also why the scale can't scare you here. A morning is water, salt and what's still in your gut — several pounds of noise around half a pound of signal. Marble watches the line through the noise, and on the morning the number jumps it tells you what it actually means.

What it asks of you

No account, no email, no sign-up — and no permissions at all when you open it for the first time.

Your weigh-ins never leave your phone. Not to a server, not to Apple Health, not anywhere — the whole trend and every correction is computed on the device. Meal photos are sent to be analysed and that's the only thing that goes out. The camera is asked for when you first take a photo, notifications only if you choose a weigh-in reminder, and nothing is asked for before the app has done something for you.

Questions

The ones worth answering honestly.

How accurate is a photo, really?

It's an estimate, and a mixed dish leaves real uncertainty about oil and portion size. But small consistent errors matter much less here than they would elsewhere: because Marble corrects your target against your actual weigh-ins, reading your meals a little low every time simply means the target settles a little lower to match — and your results still land where you wanted them. If something looks wrong, tell the coach and it re-reads the plate.

Do I have to weigh myself every day?

It's the one thing that makes the rest work, and it takes ten seconds. Weighing daily is also less stressful than weighing weekly, not more — when every reading is one dot in a line, no single morning is a verdict. You're feeding a trend, not collecting a grade.

What does it cost?

Logging is free and always unlimited — photos, weigh-ins, your trend, and the target correction. There is no cap on meals, because an incomplete record would make the correction wrong. The conversational coach is the paid part.

Do I need an account?

No. There's no sign-up, no email, and nothing to log in to. Marble asks for no system permissions at all when you first open it — the camera when you take your first photo, notifications only if you choose a weigh-in reminder.

Where does my data go?

Your weigh-ins never leave your phone — the trend and every correction are computed on the device. Meal photos are sent to be analysed, and that's the only thing that goes out. Full detail on the privacy page.

Is this a good idea if I've struggled with food?

Be careful, and talk to a doctor or a registered dietitian first. Marble is built to be less obsessive than most trackers — it hides single readings, refuses to celebrate a streak, and tells you when a scary morning is meaningless. But it is still an app that counts, and counting isn't right for everyone.
